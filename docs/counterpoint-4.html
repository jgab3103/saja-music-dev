How to write third-species counterpoint

In Fux’s third species, write four quarter notes against each whole note of the cantus firmus. This creates a continuous melodic line. The guidelines below introduce the core conventions of strict species counterpoint; some historical exceptions are more subtle than an automated checker can assess.

01 · Four against one

Write four quarter notes per cantus note, making one complete 4/4 measure. Aim for a coherent melodic line rather than four unrelated notes.

02 · The first beat

Beat 1 of each measure must be consonant with the cantus: a third, fifth, sixth, octave or permitted unison (including compound consonances).

03 · Dissonances in motion

Dissonances on beats 2, 3 or 4 may be used as passing tones, approached and left by step in the same direction. A neighbour tone leaves a consonance by step and returns by step. Their exact placement and treatment depend on the strict tradition being taught.

04 · Melodic shape

Prefer predominantly stepwise motion, with occasional carefully controlled leaps. Avoid awkward outlines, excessive repetition and leaps larger than an octave.

05 · Independence of voices

Avoid parallel perfect fifths and octaves between successive cantus downbeats. Prefer contrary or oblique motion, and avoid crossing the cantus firmus.

06 · Beginning and cadence

Start with a perfect consonance and aim for a stepwise approach to a final unison or octave. Traditional exercises often use a special closing measure rather than four quarters in the last bar; this introductory editor retains four slots throughout.

Check your work

The checker tests downbeat consonance, passing and neighbour dissonances on other beats, parallels between downbeats, melodic leaps, voice crossing, and the opening and cadence. It offers guidance, not a complete verdict on musical quality.

Third species: the essentials

Four quarter notes against each cantus whole note. Beat 1 must be consonant; dissonances on beats 2–4 require carefully controlled stepwise movement. Avoid parallel perfect fifths and octaves between successive downbeats. Shape an independent melody and end with a convincing cadence. The automated checks implement a practical subset of these rules.
