How to write fifth-species counterpoint

Fux's fifth species brings together note-against-note writing, two-against-one, four-against-one, and syncopation. The result should sound like a coherent, singable melody rather than a sequence of unrelated exercises.

1. Establish consonance

Begin and end with a perfect consonance. Consonances should underpin the structural beats; treat dissonances carefully.

2. Vary the rhythm

Combine longer and shorter note values to create a natural melodic line. Avoid changing rhythm arbitrarily in every bar.

3. Control passing dissonances

A dissonant passing note moves by step in the same direction between consonant notes. A neighbour note moves by step away and back.

4. Prepare suspensions

Tie a consonant note across the barline. If it becomes dissonant against the new cantus note, resolve it downward by step.

5. Maintain independence

Prefer contrary and oblique motion, avoid parallel perfect fifths and octaves, and keep the melodic contour singable.

6. Shape the cadence

Approach the final consonance by step. For this first version, the final measure is a whole note, without a tie into it.

Different historical presentations of fifth species vary in detail. The checker below is an instructional guide, not a complete implementation of every rule in Gradus ad Parnassum.

Check your work

Checks opening and final consonances, downbeat consonance or prepared suspensions, dissonant passing and neighbour notes, parallel perfect intervals at measure boundaries, melodic leaps, and suspension resolutions.

Fifth species: the essentials

A convincing florid line combines controlled dissonance, a clear melodic shape, rhythmic variety and a purposeful cadence. This editor offers common whole-, half- and quarter-note patterns, plus ties across barlines. More elaborate rhythmic figures and historical exceptions can be introduced in a later version.
