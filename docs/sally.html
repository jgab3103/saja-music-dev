It has been such an unusual weekend. And you have found yourself on the SAJA Music dev site, which I think needs a tad of context, so here goes.

Where to begin? You know how you sometimes say you feel like you have somehow done a bait and switch on me because you reckon you were thinner or whatever when we met? Which is ridiculous, btw, you gorgeous sex bomb.

But what is interesting about you saying that is that it is sometimes how I feel. Like when we got together, I acted pretty cool. I was the CDO guy, some cool tech mofo, and then things changed. I went from that to a career change, to no job, to no house. I guess at least I have a house again, which is cool, and I’m sorting the job now (though I worry it is taking too long). But I feel bad sometimes, thinking this is not what you signed up for, stuck with some guy going through this whole midlife thing, completely changing where he is heading and all that.

And as I have gone through this, sometimes I think I must come across as really flighty and constantly changing what I want and where I am heading. Like one day I am going to do this job or that job, or stop this or start that. I don’t really feel this way in myself, but I know I can think a million things and not really articulate them, so it could come across this way.

So I thought I should write some context down about where I think it's all heading.

Work

Firstly, the work thing. I really need to find a job (that, you know, pays more than $100 a week!). Since moving into this house, I have felt so ready to get back into life, and the work thing is a big part of it. And I want to make this place amazing and create a home for you. It is important to me. My sense is that I can work either as a teacher or some kind of tech developer. I have accepted that I just really don't want to do the managing people thing and sit in endless meetings (and maybe it's good those jobs have dried up anyway).

Work-wise, I love teaching people stuff, and I love coding away and building tech. I love making music through albums or live stuff. I don't really want to work for myself, but it's nice to feel there is a bit of a backup if I need to start teaching music from the house or something if things don't pan out soon. So there are options, and I need to stop worrying. And there seem to be jobs for teachers (once I sort the paperwork, and one panned out pretty quickly when I thought I had it sorted), as well as jobs for learning designers and web developers. They are all a good fit and mostly pretty flexible. And occasionally I see the odd data science/research job that is certainly worth checking out.

As I have tried to find something, I have also begun to realise my résumé is kind of problematic for getting some of the roles I might be interested in. The teaching thing is just a waiting game, so that is fine. But going into tech from a post-CDO job is a hard thing to sell. And so I have been thinking that I need to find some way to update my résumé so it feels like I am heading into some kind of logical narrative. I was thinking it would look far better on my résumé if I left my CDO job to go and do some kind of techy startup thing, so a move back into the tools. So it feels more like CDO leaves to do startup thing and then heads to the next thing.

So this led to me thinking that I should just build out some resources under the SAJA Music banner (which I think I mentioned), to showcase what I can do. And also I should promote BeatMatrix more, which for some reason I just haven't done.

Friday night

Anyway, this all brings me to Friday night’s dramas. It was pretty horrible. I could not sleep, completely freaking out, thinking all these things I am trying will never come to anything, and I am slowly running out of money.

So then I decided, at about 11.45, that maybe this idea of creating more resources for a website might help. But I wanted to do something more serious than a couple of small resources. So the insane thought occurred to me that I could just bring my entire codebase from years ago (that music software) into the problem, and maybe that is what SAJA Music could be. I built so much stuff in that software: tech and interactive resources, search utility algorithms over huge music datasets, all kinds of connected music history and context data. My original goal for that (SteluSa?) software was an insanely high-level digital humanities music project. I built all these different modules but never brought it together. So in a panic, I started bringing it all back to life.

So when you are checking out some of this website, it is going to be more than me creating some resources for piano students, and more like the first 5% or so my music software I wanted to build as part of my PhD. So there are some interesting things here: advanced aural training, interactive score reading and analysis, orchestration resources, interactive resources for learning counterpoint, and on it goes. As I was doing this all night, I was finding that what you can do with web audio has increased exponentially. I can create sound on the fly and serve up WAV files instantly now. I can create music voice-leading exercises that draw on an entire corpus of Bach at high speed. All the stuff that I could not get to scale, but now the tech has caught up to the ambition. Mark and I used to have these long conversations about building this stuff, and how cool it would be in advanced composition teaching environments. But I did not quite have the expertise and resources to bring it together, but now it seems to be possible.

I remember the other day we were chatting about my popping some resources up, kind of that it would help showcase skills, help provide some resources for my students, and feeling a sense that this just reinvents the wheel. But the other weird thing that surprises me is that it is actually really hard to find high-quality music resources, and most people are overwhelmed by poor information that is full of ads and paywalls and things in terrible formats. So it's interesting to have a sense that doing this is of value.

I love open source

The other thing I thought about all night (ironically, I guess, as I really need a job) is that I love the idea of creating high-quality content and making it free. I hate this thing on the internet of a million tiny apps trying to make just a couple of cents, feeling like you are selling snake oil. But when I think about the things that have changed my life, they are all completely not-for-profit. When I think about people and organisations I admire, it's Derek Sivers, Khan Academy, Paul Dawkins, Norman Wildberger, Justin Frankel, Brendan Eich. Subvert. They don't compromise; they share knowledge as a first-class citizen. They accept donations and grants, they sometimes create related collateral, but they don't withhold knowledge. And they treat the internet as it was originally intended, for the free exchange of knowledge. It is a different model, where opportunity, whether it is monetary or otherwise, does not come from pricing information, but from the opportunities that arise from sharing information with others. I like the idea that the internet is a platform for public good, and I can't stand the fact that it has been overrun by petty greed, vying for money and attention. And leaving me with a sense of not even being able to get a piece of the pie, which I can't stand anyway.

Anyway, as Friday night unfolded and I was doing all kinds of crazy code, I was also trying to come to a sense of this idea of SAJA Music, like what is the origin story? What series of events led me to freak out and try and create this? I was thinking a lot about BeatMatrix, just a tiny, cool little piece of software that for some reason I just haven't promoted. Why is that? And it is because part of me does not believe in building software that way: proprietary platforms available to those who have certain products and not others, and where I have to cede ownership and IP, and the model that I can somehow scam a couple of bucks for my app, by putting it on store whose platform has pushed me into dubious engineering decisons. And so at some point in the crazy haze of code, I ported even ported that app to the web, which I have included on this site, and then wrote a big essay about it, around coffee number 3, which seems to capture my thinking about it. It is one of the many things on this site, which is why I providing this context, so hopefully you don't tnink I have gone crazy,
