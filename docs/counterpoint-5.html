How to write fourth-species counterpoint

Fourth species uses syncopation: a note begins on the weak half of a bar and is tied into the strong half of the next bar. A dissonance created on that strong beat is a suspension. The note is prepared as a consonance, held while the cantus moves, and resolved downward by step. These are introductory Fux-style rules; exceptions depend on voice position and contrapuntal context.

01 · Rhythm

Begin with a half rest, then a half note. In the following bars, tie the second half note of the preceding bar across the barline. End on a whole note.

02 · Preparation

The note sounded on beats 3–4 must be consonant with the cantus at the moment it begins.

03 · Suspension

The tied note may become dissonant when the cantus changes on the next downbeat. Do not rearticulate it.

04 · Resolution

Resolve a dissonant suspension downward by one diatonic step on beat 3, arriving at a consonance.

05 · Consonant syncopation

A tied note that remains consonant is also possible. Its continuation should still produce a singable, independent line.

06 · Cadence

Approach the final by step and conclude on a perfect consonance, normally a unison or octave. The final measure uses a whole note.

Check your work

The checker tests downbeat consonance, passing and neighbour dissonances on other beats, parallels between downbeats, melodic leaps, voice crossing, and the opening and cadence. It offers guidance, not a complete verdict on musical quality.

Fourth species: the essentials

Start with a half rest and half note. Tie each weak-half note into the following strong half. Prepare dissonances as consonances, then resolve dissonant suspensions down by step. End on a whole note and a perfect consonance. The automated checker covers core principles but not every Fuxian exception.
