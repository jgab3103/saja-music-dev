Beginner music theory · SM_5

Notes and Bar Lines

Music is made of sounds and silences. Some sounds last a long time, and others are short. We use notes to show how long to play a sound, and bar lines to organise the beats into groups.

Three notes to learn first

Say the numbers out loud as you count each note. For now, imagine a steady beat, like walking or clapping.

Whole note

4 beats

1 2 3 4

A long sound. Hold it while you count to four.

Half note

2 beats

1 2

A medium-length sound. Hold it for two beats.

Quarter note

1 beat

1

A short sound. Play it for one beat.

Remember: One whole note lasts as long as two half notes or four quarter notes.

What is a bar line?

A bar line is a vertical line drawn through the staff. It separates music into smaller groups called bars (or measures).

Each bar above contains four quarter-note beats. The vertical line in the middle separates the two bars.

When music is grouped into four beats per bar, you count 1, 2, 3, 4 and then start again at 1 in the next bar. Other music can have different numbers of beats per bar; we'll learn about those later.
